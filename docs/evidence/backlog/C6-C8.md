## C6: sample is the fastest-varying index

`rational_forward_kernel` enumerated `index = sample*outputs + o`: consecutive lanes held
different outputs of one sample, so the edge-major caches `[edge*capacity + sample]`
(`P`, `Q`, `dr/dx`, safe policies also `g`) were written with stride `inputs*capacity`, and
the parameters of different edges were read by neighbouring lanes. Now `index = o*batch +
sample`: a warp holds 32 consecutive samples of one output, cache writes are coalesced and
the edge's parameters are a warp-uniform broadcast. Each thread still sums its output over
the inputs in order, so the result is bitwise unchanged. The output write
`output[sample*outputs + o]` is now strided, one value per thread against `3-4*inputs`
coalesced cache values; staging it through shared memory was not worth a block-level
transposition (the remaining forward cost is arithmetic, below).

`rational_input_kernel` read `derivatives[(o*inputs+i)*capacity + sample]` with
`index = sample*inputs + i` (stride `capacity` across lanes); it now enumerates
`(input, sample)` with the sample fastest, same summation order.

## C7: parameter-VJP checks in the forward pass

### What the contract requires

The forward pass evaluated, for every executed sample and edge, all `max(m,n)+1` powers,
quotients `z^k/Q` and numerator/denominator VJPs only to report nonfinite values. This is
not incidental:

- CONTRACT (M4, M2): "Nonfinite intermediates/results raise `std::overflow_error`", "all
  finiteness checks remain (... every derivative intermediate)", the guard applies "for every
  executed sample/edge ... including zero numerators and zero upstreams".
- The CPU `Layer::forward` evaluates them (`evaluate_rational_trusted`) and raises during
  forward; `ResidentNetwork::forward()` matches it today.
- `rational_parameter_kernel` recomputes them with the identity guard `CheckedEarlier`,
  relying on the forward having checked them (R3).
- C9: a training step attributes the failure to its forward phase; with a guarded pole in the
  same step, the forward word holds both bits and the step raises `overflow_error`, as
  `forward()` would.

Moving the checks into the parameter-VJP kernel (the brief's first option) would make
resident `forward()` succeed where the CPU forward raises, change which eager call raises
and the phase (and, combined with a pole, the exception type) a training step reports, and
put the guards back into the reduction loop that R3 had to strip (+17.7%). That is a
contract change with a CPU/GPU divergence. The exact cheap equivalent was preferred, and the
contract is unchanged.

### The bound

`rational_parameter_vjps_bounded` (`src/detail/rational_formulas.hpp`) is a sufficient
condition for the full check (`rational_parameter_vjps_check`, the former loop, unchanged)
to find every intermediate finite. The forward runs the full check only where the bound
fails, so the reported status is exactly the full check's in every case:

```
power   = |z|^max(m,n) by repeated multiplication if |z| > 1, else 1
d       = power if |Q| >= 1, else power/|Q|          (at most one division)
require d <= limit                                     (limit = max/4 of the precision)
n > 0:  s = d, Smooth: s = fl(|g| d) and s <= limit
        require fl(|r| s) <= limit                     (r = P/Q, already computed)
```

Why it is sufficient (IEEE round to nearest is monotone and sign-symmetric):

1. The check forms `z^k` by repeated multiplication. For `|z| >= 1`, `|fl(p z)| >= |p|`, so
   `|z^k|` as rounded is nondecreasing and `power` reproduces the largest one exactly; for
   `|z| < 1` all are at most 1. So every `|z^k| <= power`, and every power is finite.
2. `|fl(z^k/Q)| <= fl(power/|Q|) = d` (for `|Q| >= 1` already `<= |z^k| <= power`).
3. Ordinary paths: Guarded `|fl(-r z^k/Q)| <= fl(|r| d)`; Absolute evaluates that Guarded
   form first (all its guards) and multiplies by `g` in `{-1, 0, 1}`; Smooth
   `|fl(g z^k/Q)| <= fl(|g| d) = s` and `|fl(-r s_k)| <= fl(|r| s)`. Numerator VJPs are the
   quotients of 2.
4. Log-space paths run only when one factor (`z^k`, `z^k/Q`, `r`, Smooth `g` or `g z^k/Q`) is
   below the normal range. They return `exp` of the exact log of the product of the other
   factors, accurate to far better than the factor 4: if every factor is normal, the product
   is at most `limit*(1 + O(17 u))`; if one is tiny, the product is at most about
   `2^-1022 * limit` or `2^-1022 * DBL_MAX` (FP32: `2^-126 ...`), i.e. a few units. The
   numerator path with a tiny `z^k` is at most `2^-1022/|Q|`, and a non-pole `Q` is nonzero, so
   at most `2^52` (FP32 `2^23`). All finite.
5. A pole or a nonfinite `z`/`Q` fails the bound (`NaN`/`inf` comparisons are false), and the
   full check then behaves as before.

FMA builds contract none of the bound's or the check's products (no adds follow them) except
inside the log-space exponents, which the margin covers.

Randomized verification ([c7_bound_fuzz.cpp](C6-C8-bench/c7_bound_fuzz.cpp), host build of the
shared formulas, degrees 0..16, coefficients and inputs log-uniform over the whole exponent
range in four scale regimes, all policies, FP64 and FP32): 8.33 M evaluated samples, the bound
held for 8.18 M and the check reported nothing in any of them (0 violations); among the
samples where the bound failed, 124 k had a clean check and 30 k a reporting one, so both
sides of the threshold were exercised.

Cost per sample and edge: at most `max(m,n)` multiplications, one division (Guarded with
`|Q| < 1` only), two products and three comparisons, instead of `max(m,n)+1` divisions, about
`3(m+n)` products and the log-space tests. ncu (same step as above): FP64 forward 116.4 ->
79.7 ms, FP32 6.72 -> 4.49 ms per wide layer.

## C8: one warp per edge in the parameter VJP

`rational_parameter_kernel` ran one warp per parameter (`m+1` numerators, `n` denominators per
edge, one per bias): each warp reread the layer input, the upstream and the cached `P`, `Q`,
`g` of every sample and recomputed `z` and `z^k` (plus `P/Q` per denominator parameter). Now one
warp owns an edge: per sample it loads `u`, `z`, `Q`, `P`, `g` once, forms `P/Q` once and the
powers incrementally, and accumulates all `m+1+n` sums in registers (fixed-size arrays of 17 and
16 with unrolled, constant-indexed loops: 68-73 registers FP32, 120-126 FP64, no local
memory); the warp of edge `(o, 0)` also accumulates the bias of output `o`. Lane `l` still sums
samples `l, l+32, ...` in order and each sum is reduced by the same `shfl_down` tree
(offsets 16..1), so every gradient is bitwise the former kernel's.

Library primitives (AGENTS rule 8):

- cuBLAS: the parameter VJPs are not a matrix product. `dr/da_k = z^k/Q` and
  `dr/db_k = -r g z^k/Q` depend on the edge and the sample, so `sum_s u[s,o] * f(o,i,s,k)` has
  no GEMM factorization. Only the bias (`db = U^T 1`) is a GEMV, as in `expansion_backward`;
  it is fused here for free into the warps that already read `u`, and a cuBLAS GEMV would add a
  launch and change the summation order.
- CUB `WarpReduce<T>::Sum` reduces with ascending offsets (1, 2, 4, 8, 16:
  `warp_reduce_shfl.cuh`, `ReduceStep(..., 1 << STEP)`), a different association than the
  existing tree, so it would change every gradient's bits; for `m+n+1` independent sums it
  offers nothing a five-step shuffle loop does not. The hand-written reduction is kept.

ncu: FP64 121.0 -> 61.1 ms, FP32 15.2 -> 5.3-6.6 ms per wide layer.

## Profiling follow-ups (AGENTS rule 6)

After C6-C8 the FP64 kernels were FP64-pipe bound (85-87%) and the FP32 forward issue bound.
Three further steps, each bitwise neutral:

1. **Integer finiteness tests (`25f6200`).** `isfinite(double)` and `|v| < min_normal` compile
   to `DSETP`, issued on GA102's 1/64-rate FP64 pipe; every status guard of every Horner step
   and log-space test cost an FP64 slot. Device code now tests the exponent field
   (`(__double2hiint(v) & 0x7ff00000) != 0x7ff00000`, `== 0` for tiny): `report`/`finite_value`
   in `resident.cu`, `math::finite`/`math::tiny` in `host_device.hpp` (host code unchanged; this
   also covers the basis formulas). FP64 forward 79.7 -> 59.2 ms, parameter VJP 61.1 -> 46.1 ms.
   In the FMA build this let nvcc contract Smooth's `Q = 1 + S*S` into `fma(S, S, 1)` (the
   rational dump moved by up to 1.1e-14); `rounded_product` now rounds that checked product on
   its own in every build, as before and as the CPU, and the dump is identical again.
2. **Argument cache (`9d37b5d`).** `z = (x - center)/scale` depends only on input and sample,
   but the forward divided it once per output and the parameter VJP once per edge, both reading
   the layer input with stride `inputs`. `rational_argument_kernel` writes it once (with the
   forward's guard, same phase) to a `capacity*inputs` arena region; the forward
   (`rational_horner_at`) and parameter kernels read it coalesced. The input-VJP and
   parameter-VJP loops are unrolled (8, 2) for memory-level parallelism; addition order
   unchanged. FP64 forward 59.2 -> 53.2, parameter VJP 46.1 -> 40.1 ms; FP32 forward
   4.49 -> 4.19, parameter VJP 5.3-6.6 -> 3.6 ms, input VJP 1.26 -> 1.22 ms.
3. **One guard per Horner chain (`29b9742`).** `x*z + c` of a nonfinite `x` is nonfinite
   (`inf*0`, `inf - inf` are NaN), so a chain whose intermediate overflows ends nonfinite;
   guarding the final `P`, `P'`, `Q`, `Q'`, pole bound, `S`, `S'` reports exactly what a guard per
   step did. The CPU raises the same `overflow_error` after the loop instead of inside it. FP32
   forward 4.19 -> 3.50 ms; FP64 unchanged (pipe bound).

Rejected: shared-memory transposition of the forward output and of the input-VJP upstream
(their traffic is a few percent of the kernels'); splitting the batch of an edge over several
warps (would change the summation order); computing `1/Q` once and multiplying (changes results,
see open questions).

## Contract

No behavior changes: the same values bitwise, the same exceptions from the same calls and
phases. [CONTRACT.md](../../CONTRACT.md) now describes the resident rational layout (the new
`capacity*inputs` argument region per rational layer, inside the construction-time arena;
`workspace_allocations()` unchanged), that the forward pass reports nonfinite parameter-VJP
intermediates as on the CPU with the bound-screened exact check, the end-of-chain Horner check,
and the per-edge parameter VJP.

## Deviations from the brief

1. **C7 keeps forward-time detection** (no contract change) instead of moving the checks into the
   parameter-VJP kernel; reasons above. The brief allowed this as the fallback.
2. **Three profiling follow-ups beyond C6-C8** (integer finiteness tests, argument cache, one
   guard per Horner chain). They are separate commits, bitwise neutral, and come from the rule-6
   profile of the restructured kernels. The integer tests touch `host_device.hpp`, outside the
   files the brief assigned, and also speed up the FP64 basis kernels (frozen m2/m3 above).
3. **Wide topology at batch 2048** for FP64 and FP32, plus FP32 at 8192: the rational caches are
   `3-4 * batch * edges` values, 26-35 GB for FP64 256->256->256->10 at 8192, more than the 24 GB
   device. FP32 at 8192 is measured (13-17.5 GB).
4. **Small learning rate (1e-5) in the step benchmark**: with a fixed upstream, larger rates drive
   the Guarded FP32 denominators into a pole within the timed steps; the work per step is the
   same.
5. **No shared-memory staging** of the forward output or the input-VJP upstream (measured to be
   a few percent of traffic; the remaining forward cost is arithmetic).

## Open questions for the owner

- **Reciprocal of `Q`.** The FP64 rational forward and parameter VJP are now FP64-pipe bound
  (87-89%) by IEEE divisions: `P/Q`, `P'/Q`, `Q'/Q` in the forward, `P/Q` and `z^k/Q` for
  `k <= max(m,n)` in the VJP. Computing `1/Q` once and multiplying would remove most of them
  (estimated 30-40% of these kernels) but changes every quotient by up to one ulp, so results
  would no longer be bitwise those of the CPU formulas (still within the resident tolerance).
  Adopt as an FP64/FP32 performance option, or keep exact quotients?
- **CPU forward.** The CPU `Layer::forward` also evaluates every parameter VJP
  (`evaluate_rational_trusted`) only to check it; the same bound could skip that work on the CPU.
  Not done here (CPU sources outside this pass, besides the shared formulas).
