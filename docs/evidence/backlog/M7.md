## Scope decisions (AGENTS rule 5)

1. **Per-edge parameters, as the backlog states.** Each wavelet has its own `tau` and `l`. The
   alternative, per-input or per-term parameters shared by a layer, keeps a GEMM form but is a
   Mexican-hat variant of `TrainableRbfEdges` (shared centers and widths), not Wav-KAN: every
   edge's function shape would still come from the same dictionary. The cost of the per-edge form
   (no GEMM, one evaluation per sample, output, input and term) is measured below rather than
   avoided.
2. **Hybrid: `size >= 1` wavelets per edge.** `size = 1` is Wav-KAN's edge; `size > 1` is the M3
   per-layer dictionary made per-edge and trainable (the M3 evidence's dictionary of fixed
   wavelets is what the finding names). One carrier covers both; the per-edge coefficient tensor
   keeps the carrier convention `(outputs, inputs, terms)` that L2, `set_parameters` and the
   initializers act on.
3. **Normalization `A s^(-1/2)` (the repository's), not Wav-KAN's unnormalized `psi((x - tau)/s)`.**
   The factor is a reparametrization of `w` (Wav-KAN's code multiplies by a free weight anyway);
   keeping it makes the term identical to `MexicanHatConfig`'s, reuses its moments, its tail
   conventions and its tests. It changes `d/dl` by `-psi/2`. Wav-KAN's code also uses the sign
   `(q^2 - 1)`; the sign is absorbed in `w`.
4. **Log scale `l`, `s = exp(l)`**, as the RBF log widths: positivity by construction; a log
   scale is valid when `exp(l)` and `exp(-l)` are finite and nonzero (|l| below ~709.78; FP32
   ~88.7 on the resident executor). An SGD candidate outside raises `overflow_error` (CPU) or fails
   the step with rollback (device).
5. **Configuration = term count only; tau and l are state** (as rational denominators): `operator==`
   of `WaveletConfig` is the structure `upload_parameters` checks; tau and l are uploaded.
6. **Constructor placement** `tau_k = -1 + (2k+1)/size`, `s = 1/size`: equispaced hats whose zero
   crossings meet, `size = 1` gives Wav-KAN's initialization (`tau = 0, s = 1`). Distinct starting
   tau are necessary: equal terms of an edge receive equal gradients and stay equal.
7. **Initializers keep tau and l** (they place the wavelets, as RBF centers), draw only
   coefficients (then the residual weights, as for every carrier).

## Design

### Formula and tails (`src/carriers/local/local_formulas.hpp`)

`wavelet_scales(l)` gives `r = exp(-l)` and `a = A sqrt(r)` once per wavelet (per call on the
CPU, per forward on the device), so an evaluation costs one exponential. `wavelet_terms` computes
`q = (x - tau) r`, `E`, and the direct terms
`psi = a((1 - q^2)E)`, `dpsi/dx = (a(q(q^2 - 3)E)) r`, `dpsi/dl = -(a((q^2 - r1)(q^2 - r2)E))`
(polynomial factors times E are bounded, `a` and `r` multiply last; the factored log-scale
polynomial, roots `(7 +- sqrt 41)/4`, keeps relative accuracy near its zeros). When `E` is below
the normal range or `q^2` is infinite, the out-of-line `wavelet_tail_terms` (KAN_NOINLINE)
retries an overflowed distance as `x r - tau r`, returns the exact zero tail, or forms all three
terms in log space. Long-double checks: relative error `1e-15 (8 + q^2)` (rounding of `q^2/2`)
with an absolute floor `1e-15 a` near polynomial zeros; the narrow-wavelet tail matches its
log-space closed form to 1e-11.

### CPU (`src/backends/cpu/wavelet_edges.cpp`)

Loops over samples, outputs, inputs, terms; forward from the bias in ascending i, k; backward
accumulates `dx`, `dw`, `dtau`, `dl` (order in CONTRACT). Validation in `validate`,
`require_finite_nonlinear`, SGD candidate validation in `update_nonlinear` (R2 protocol: Layer
and Network unchanged apart from the constructor).

### Resident CUDA (`src/backends/cuda/resident.cu`)

There is no GEMM to delegate to cuBLAS (each edge's terms are its own; caching per-sample rows
would need `capacity*outputs*inputs*size` values), so the carrier has custom kernels (AGENTS
rule 8: no library facility covers the operation):

- forward: `wavelet_scales_kernel`, a 32x32 tiled transpose of the layer input to
  `xt [i*capacity + b]`, `wavelet_forward_kernel` (block = output x sample chunk x input split; thread = S
  samples, lanes consecutive samples: coalesced `xt` reads; parameters at block-uniform
  addresses; unsplit, the sum from the bias in the CPU order);
- backward: transpose of the upstream to `ut`, `wavelet_bias_kernel`, one fused
  `wavelet_backward_kernel` (block = input x partial slot x output split) that evaluates each
  sample and wavelet once for dx and the three parameter sums (separate dx and parameter kernels
  would evaluate twice), reducing each wavelet's sums by a fixed butterfly and over the warps in
  order into its slot; `wavelet_input_finish_kernel` (splits) and `wavelet_finish_kernel` (slots,
  `+ lambda w`). No float atomics; double-buffered shared sums need one barrier per round of 8
  wavelets. The round loop is not unrolled (its body is already S evaluations); the wavelet index
  (o, k, j) is carried from pair to pair (no 64-bit division), and the per-round partial
  addresses, the plane stride and the dx offset live in shared memory, so no 64-bit address is
  live across the evaluation loop;
- forward input splits (review L3): a layer with few outputs has few (output, chunk) blocks;
  grid z then splits its inputs, each split sums its inputs from zero into a partial workspace and
  `wavelet_forward_finish_kernel` adds bias + split 0 + split 1 + ... in order (deterministic; the
  unsplit forward keeps the CPU order bitwise).

### Launch model ([launch-model/](M7-bench/launch-model/), [sweep-1.csv](M7-bench/sweep-1.csv), [sweep-2.csv](M7-bench/sweep-2.csv), [run_sweep.sh](M7-bench/run_sweep.sh))

The first version chose S by fixed batch thresholds (`256 S·15/16 <= batch`, FP32 S = 8 -> 4
below one wave of forward blocks, backward S halved while `inputs·chunks` was below half a wave).
The review measured it up to 10% off the best forced S just above chunk multiples (FP64 b1025,
FP32 64-wide b512/b1000) and found narrow output layers run with few blocks (256->10 forward: 20
blocks for a wave of 144). It is replaced by a cost model (CONTRACT "Launch geometry"):

- *Per-kernel probe.* [kprobe.cpp](M7-bench/launch-model/kprobe.cpp) times the forward and the
  backward kernel (+ their reduction kernels) under nsys with NVTX ranges for 3080 forced
  geometries ([kcfg-f32.txt](M7-bench/launch-model/kcfg-f32.txt), [kcfg-f64.txt](M7-bench/launch-model/kcfg-f64.txt):
  11 FP32 and 7 FP64 layer shapes, 64->64, 64->8, 256->256, 256->10, 4->256, 256->1, 16->512,
  512->16, 1024->1024, K = 1 and 3, batch 256-8192, every S and split count);
  [extract.py](M7-bench/launch-model/extract.py) gives [kf32.json](M7-bench/launch-model/kf32.json) and
  [kf64.json](M7-bench/launch-model/kf64.json), 1 us added per extra kernel (graph launch gap).
- *Model.* For S and splits z: `(max(1, b/n) + 0.5)·(c/slots·it·max(n·(S + 3), 20) + 50·n)` + 100
  for the reduction kernel of a split, b blocks per multiprocessor, n = min(⌈b⌉, resident blocks
  of the instantiation): issue-bound with enough resident warps, latency-bound otherwise, idle
  sample slots of a partial chunk cost as much as busy ones (they stay in the unrolled code). The
  five constants were fitted by grid search ([model2.py](M7-bench/launch-model/model2.py),
  [fit2.py](M7-bench/launch-model/fit2.py)) on GB10; they are relative costs. Multiprocessors and
  the resident blocks of every instantiation are queried from the CUDA runtime at construction,
  so the choice adapts to the device: on an RTX 3090 the estimate uses its 82 multiprocessors
  and its occupancy queried per instantiation (the FP32 backward is bounded to 3 blocks on both;
  forward instantiations differ, e.g. S = 8: 59 registers, 4 blocks on sm_86 against 74, 3 on
  GB10, [ptxas.txt](M7-bench/ptxas.txt)); it does not promise to fill every multiprocessor (where one block per
  multiprocessor is latency-bound anyway, fewer, larger splits may win). It has not been
  measured on an sm_86 device (none available).
- *Kernel-level regret* ([evaluate.py](M7-bench/launch-model/evaluate.py), [fit.txt](M7-bench/launch-model/fit.txt);

The dictionary evaluates `batch*inputs*terms` basis values and contracts them with cuBLAS
(tensor cores in TF32); the per-edge carrier evaluates `batch*outputs*inputs*K` wavelets twice
per step with no shared expansion, so the ratio grows with the output width (1024-wide: 5.5x).
This is inherent to per-edge parameters (scope decision 1). Narrow wavelets whose warps mostly
hold log-space samples cost more per evaluation: FP32 64x64x64x8, K = 4/16/32 placements
(s = 1/4, 1/16, 1/32 on [-1, 1]): 0.601/7.34/18.4 ms, i.e. 3.1x and 3.8x the evaluation cost at
K = 4 (the K = 16 and 32 rows of the table; the K = 4 step became 10% faster in the review).
Against the dictionary at equal parameters per edge: FP32 1.3-5.5x the step outside these narrow
placements (2.9-3.1x at 256 wide, 3.1-3.3x for them), TF32 4.1x, FP64 3.3-6.6x.

## Demonstration of benefit ([demo.csv](M7-bench/demo.csv), [wavelet_demo.cpp](M7-bench/wavelet_demo.cpp))

against the 3K-term fixed dictionary (same placement rule), plus the dictionary's global

With localized features off the dictionary grid, trained translations and scales give 1.5-2.7x
lower held-out error than the best any optimizer can reach with the fixed dictionary at the best

## Open questions for the owner

- FP64 wavelet layers are FP64-exponential bound on GB10 (6.6x the fixed dictionary's step at 256
  wide). An FP64 envelope through a cheaper exponential would break bitwise-close parity with the
  CPU formula; not done.
- The log-space tail is exact but costs about 3-4x per evaluation when narrow wavelets put most
  samples there; a cheaper scaled-exponent tail for moderate `q^2` is possible.
- The launch cost model's five relative costs are GB10 fits; the device enters through its
  multiprocessors and each instantiation's occupancy (CUDA runtime), and sm_86 compiles without
  spills (its forward instantiations reach different occupancies than on GB10), but no sm_86 device was available to measure. Other GPUs keep
  correct, deterministic results; re-running the probe ([launch-model/](M7-bench/launch-model/))
  on an RTX 3090 would confirm or refit the constants.
- A sample's forward output depends (in rounding only) on the batch and the device: they decide
  the input splits and with them the summation order. Results are bitwise reproducible for one
  device, build and batch, as for every resident carrier with batch-dependent geometry.
